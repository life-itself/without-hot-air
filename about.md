# About this edition

> **This is not the official edition.** David MacKay's original book and website
> live at [withouthotair.com](https://withouthotair.com). This is a community
> edition: the same text, converted to an editable, open format, so that it can
> be corrected, extended and eventually brought up to date by whoever wants to
> help.

## Why this project

David MacKay tragically passed away in 2016. His wonderful book (and website) were first published in 2008. The material is still of great value. However, it has not been updated since publication and now won't be (at least by David). David made his book available in machine readable formats and licensed the book (semi-)openly, meaning it can be modified and redistributed.

The book remains a go-to reference and extremely useful. However it is getting out of date — the solar situation today is dramatically different from that of 2008.[^1] Its original format is not easily editable, nor is it in a repository where it can be collaboratively worked on. Finally, there is the risk the book and site could one day disappear.

So this project does three things:

* **Converts** the book into markdown — a source form that is much easier to edit, reuse and publish
* **Opens** it, in a public [git repository][git], so anyone can propose a correction or an update
* **Preserves** it, archiving the original material alongside the converted text

## What this makes possible

* A revised edition, built collaboratively, bringing the numbers up to date
* Easier reuse and citation by others
* Additional functionality over time — annotation, search, better navigation

## What we have *not* done

We have deliberately **not** changed MacKay's text. Every chapter here is his,
as published in 2008. Where the numbers have dated — and in energy, many have —
they are still his numbers, not ours. Revising them is the work ahead, and when
it happens it should be visible and attributed, not smuggled in.

That is also why the figures, the arithmetic and the arguments are left intact
even where a modern reader would quibble. The value of the book was always the
method: work it out yourself, in units you can hold in your head. That survives
the numbers going stale.

## How to help

* **Spot an error, or a number that has dated?** Use the **Edit this page**
  link at the bottom of the page — it opens that chapter's markdown on GitHub,
  ready to edit, and turns your change into a pull request.
* **Rather discuss it first?** Open [an issue][issues], or
  [get in touch](https://lifeitself.org/contact).
* **Working on something bigger?** The whole book is markdown in the
  [repo][git]; clone it and send a pull request.
